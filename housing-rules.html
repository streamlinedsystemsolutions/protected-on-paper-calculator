What does the “5% medical deduction” mean?

This guide is available throughout Colorado. You do not need to live in a particular county or pass our income checker to read it.

Educational public preview.

This explains HUD-assisted housing deductions. It does not establish your eligibility, change your official rent, or replace a written decision from the agency administering your housing.

Start with your housing program

Ask which program pays your assistance and which agency administers it. An ordinary private-market lease, a housing voucher, public housing and a HUD-assisted apartment do not all use the same rent calculation.

The medical-expense thresholds discussed here come from federal HUD rules. Colorado agencies administer programs using the rules and policies that apply to them. The explanation should be available statewide; applying 5% to every low-income household would give some people the wrong result.

The percentage is an expense threshold

It is not a promise to reduce your rent by that percentage. Under the applicable deduction rules, the agency first determines which unreimbursed expenses qualify. It then subtracts the applicable percentage of annual income from those eligible annual expenses. A positive remainder may reduce adjusted annual income.

Medical deductions have household eligibility requirements. Disability-related attendant care and equipment have their own conditions. Do not assume every bill qualifies or count an expense that insurance or another source reimburses.

Thresholds explained — confirm which one applies
ThresholdMeaning
5%May apply through qualifying phased-in relief or an approved general hardship. It is not the default for everyone.
7.5%The second phase-in step for a qualifying household.
10%The general threshold under the applicable HOTMA medical/disability expense rule.
3%A historical/pre-transition comparison; confirm the program's effective policy.

7% is not the federal phase-in step. If a calculator offers it, it must be treated as a custom hypothetical.

Confirm your housing agency

Before using the examples: check the exact agency name. Boulder Housing Partners (BHP) and Boulder County Housing Authority (BCHA) are different agencies. BHP’s July 2026 voucher plan, section 6-III says its Moving to Work authority removes mandatory deductions and it does not offer permissive deductions. The standard medical-deduction examples below may therefore not apply to a BHP household. BCHA’s published 2026 plan also describes an alternative rent method and hardship provisions, but its medical-expense sections contain differing implementation dates and conditions. Do not choose a date or formula from this guide to resolve that difference. Ask your own agency for the policy section, effective date, household category and written worksheet it used. A calculator’s standard HUD learning example does not confirm that your agency uses it. Agency sources reviewed November 16, 2026; individual applicability remains unverified.

A fictional example

Suppose an agency has approved $24,000 in annual income and $4,200 in eligible annual expenses.

10%$2,400$1,800

An annual deduction is not a monthly rent credit. If the 30%-of-adjusted-monthly-income component controls, an extra $1,200 annual deduction reduces that component by $30 per month. Other rent components, utility allowances and program policies can change the final tenant payment.

Confirm the effective policy before calculating

HUD Notice PIH 2026-15 sets January 1, 2027 compliance requirements for covered public housing agencies, with exceptions for specified Moving to Work and reporting-system agencies. Separate housing programs can follow different implementation instructions.

HUD's September 29, 2026 FAQ describes a planned change to phase-in eligibility. The FAQ says that change depends on a final rule becoming effective. Do not treat a proposed change as an already effective rule. Ask your administrator for the current written policy and the rule effective on your review date.

For a Moving to Work program or an uncertain policy, use this explanation to ask questions rather than assuming the generic estimate applies. The assistance checker's policy gate remains in place.

Ask for the calculation in writing

Please provide the calculation for my current review: the income period and annual income used; each expense and deduction allowed or denied and why; the medical-expense threshold and its effective rule; any phase-in or hardship decision and its start/end dates; the rent components, utility allowance, tenant portion and effective date; and how I can request a correction or hearing. Please identify the current administrative-plan section used.

Keep copies of what you send and receive. A calculator result does not change an agency notice or extend a deadline.
