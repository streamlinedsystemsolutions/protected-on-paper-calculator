Estimate a Housing Choice Voucher medical deduction

This is a standard HUD learning illustration for an existing voucher household whose agency uses these deduction rules reviewing its rent calculation. It does not decide if you qualify for a voucher or for a medical deduction.

Check who can claim a deduction: Under the federal rule, the medical-care expense deduction generally applies to an eligible elderly or disabled family. Certain unreimbursed attendant-care or disability equipment expenses may also count when they enable a family member to work, under additional conditions. Do not enter every medical bill automatically or assume that a matching number means your expenses qualify. Read the federal rule (24 CFR 5.611).

🔒
Private, temporary math. Amounts stay in this page while it is open. They are not saved, uploaded or used for applications. Your housing agency must confirm whether these deduction rules apply, the correct threshold, eligible expenses, other deductions and actual rent. Moving to Work agencies can use different rules.

Important: 5% is a learning default here, not the legally required rate for everyone. HUD's transitional steps are 5%, 7.5%, and 10%, with a former 3% rate and hardship exceptions. The 7% option is a custom hypothetical for comparison, not an official HUD medical threshold or phase-in step. This rate is not the percentage of rent you pay. A higher rate can mean a smaller medical-expense deduction. The amounts for dependent and elderly/disabled allowances may be adjusted by HUD; enter only the amount confirmed by your administrator.

Show how this estimate is calculated
  1. Start with the annual income your housing agency counts (before deductions).
  2. Multiply that income by your agency-confirmed medical threshold (such as 10%, or a lower approved hardship/phase-in rate).
  3. Subtract that threshold amount from eligible, unreimbursed yearly expenses. If the answer is negative, the medical deduction is zero.
  4. Subtract the approved medical deduction and other agency-approved yearly deductions from yearly income.
  5. Divide adjusted income by 12. Compare 30% of adjusted monthly income with 10% of gross monthly income and any applicable minimum rent. The highest applicable figure is a simplified total tenant payment, not necessarily the amount owed to the landlord.

This example does not include a possible welfare-rent comparison, utility allowances, payment standards, property rent, or special rules. Ask for your agency's written calculation before making a financial decision.
